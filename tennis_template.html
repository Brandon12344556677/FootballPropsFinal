⚡ Value spots

match winners: market gives it 30-80%, model is 8-20 points better
What counts as a tennis value spot: all three rules have to be true
  1. 1The market gives it 30% to 80%. The price you'd pay has to be 30¢ to 80¢. No longshots, and no heavy favorites that pay almost nothing.
  2. 2Our model puts it 8 to 20 points higher. 40¢ ask → needs 48-60% · 60¢ → 68-80%. A bigger gap against a busy tennis market is almost always news the ratings can't see: an injury, a withdrawal, a player carrying a knock. The gap is the edge on each card.
  3. 3It's a match winner. Set winners, exact scores, game spreads and total games are priced on every match and graded in Past picks, but they don't make this list until they've shown a record: on the backtest the totals were barely better than a coin flip.
The rules are tested against the cheaper of the Polymarket and Kalshi prices for that side with at least $25 offered at it (or within 2¢), the price you'd actually pay. Never both players in one match. Both players need 8+ matches in the data. Tennis starts are often later than listed (a match "follows" the one before it on that court), so a pick can lock well before the first ball. New sport: the tennis model has a backtest behind it but no record against prices yet, so treat it as a test until Past picks has a few hundred graded.
